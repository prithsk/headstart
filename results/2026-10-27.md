# headstart on AMI meeting questions, 2026-10-27

200 real questions (Elicit-Inform dialogue acts) from the AMI Meeting Corpus, replayed at their recorded word times. Answers from `gpt-4o-mini` with real time to first token; equivalence judged by `gpt-4o-mini`. Main setting: transcription delay 300 ms (Cluely's quoted figure), endpoint silence 800 ms, soft pause 250 ms.

| Question end to first answer on screen | p50 | p90 |
|---|---|---|
| Baseline: wait for endpoint silence, then ask | 1.33 s | 1.55 s |
| Speculative: start answers while the question is still being asked, show the newest | 0.81 s | 1.02 s |
| Ablation: same, but show any ready answer, even to an older version of the question | 0.69 s | 0.95 s |

**Median wait cut from 1.33 s to 0.81 s, 1.6x faster.**

Premature answers, shown during a pause before the speaker finished the question: baseline 2% of questions, speculative 2%. This is the price of answering on shorter pauses.

## Answer quality

Early answers, shown before the newest full-question answer existed: main rule 16 of 200, 5 (31%) rated as useful as the full-question answer. Ablation rule 91 of 200, 20 (22%) rated as useful.

Answers shown before the full question was transcribed: 16 of 200. The judge rated 5 (31%) as useful as the full-question answer; the rest would be revised on screen.
On the 90 specific questions (excluding 110 the judge flagged as vague, like "what do you think?"): 1 of 3 early answers (33%) rated as useful.

Judge controls on 100 questions: it rated identical answers the same 96% of the time, and an answer to a different question the same 0% of the time.
Model calls per question: 3.3 on average, against 1 for the baseline.

## Honest limits

- This compares two policies under the same assumptions. It does not measure Cluely's product; CALIBRATE.md describes how to time Cluely itself on the same questions.
- Word timings come from forced alignment of headset audio, and transcription delay is modeled as fixed, so this assumes a good streaming transcriber in a quiet room.
- Time to first token is measured one call at a time. Concurrent speculative calls in a real app could slow each other.
- An early answer rated worse is replaced when the full-question answer arrives, so it costs a visible revision, not a wrong final answer.
- Preemptive generation exists in voice-agent frameworks such as LiveKit Agents. The contribution here is measuring it for a meeting copilot on real meeting questions, with an answer-quality check.
- AMI meetings are scripted design meetings with mostly non-native English speakers.
